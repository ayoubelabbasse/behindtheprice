You already paid at Costco.

The cashier scanned everything. Your card went through.

So why are you stopped again at the door?

Most people assume theft.

Costco's own explanation is different.

It says the receipt check verifies purchases were processed correctly, keeps inventory accurate, and helps make sure members were charged properly.

Think about one case of drinks under the cart.

If it isn't scanned, your bill is low — but Costco's computer still thinks that case is inside the warehouse.

If something gets scanned twice, inventory may be fine — but you overpaid.

That tiny checkpoint catches mismatches before both sides leave.

And at Costco's scale, accuracy matters.

In fiscal 2026: more than 297 billion dollars in net sales, 84.1 million paid members, and a 92.3 percent U.S. and Canada renewal rate.

So the marker at the door isn't just a random ritual.

It's a tiny control point inside a massive inventory and membership machine.
