# 001 — Costco Receipt

## Working title

**Why Costco Checks Your Receipt Before You Leave**

Thumbnail promise: **WHY CHECK IT?**

## Editorial thesis

Costco's exit receipt check is not best explained as a simple "we think you stole something" ritual. Costco's own customer-service page says the check is used to confirm purchases were processed correctly, maintain inventory accuracy, and ensure members were charged properly.

The larger business story is that Costco runs an unusually high-volume, rapid-turnover retail model at comparatively low merchandise gross margins, while membership fees and renewal are strategically important. A cheap second control point at the door fits that system: catch transaction mismatches before the member leaves, help protect inventory records, and reinforce confidence in a paid membership experience.

Do not claim theft is irrelevant. Costco has separate loss-prevention and security functions. The safe wording is: **Costco's stated reason for the receipt check centers on transaction and inventory accuracy, not simply theft prevention.**

## Locked claims

### C01 — Costco checks receipts at warehouse exits
Costco says receipt verification is standard practice at its warehouse locations.

**Source:** S01  
**Confidence:** High

### C02 — Costco's stated reasons are transaction accuracy, inventory accuracy, and correct member charges
Costco says it double-checks that purchases were correctly processed by cashiers, calls the check its most effective method of maintaining inventory-control accuracy, and says it helps ensure members were charged properly.

**Source:** S01, S02  
**Confidence:** High

### C03 — Receipt inspection is part of the membership conditions
Costco's current Member Privileges and Conditions say receipts and merchandise will be inspected as members leave the warehouse to ensure members are correctly charged.

The same policy separately reserves Costco's right to inspect bags/containers. Keep those clauses distinct.

**Source:** S02  
**Confidence:** High

### C04 — Costco has dedicated member-service and loss-prevention functions
A current Costco Member Service Assistant job description includes checking receipts when members exit and performing safety/security checks. Costco also lists a separate Loss Prevention Clerk role whose stated purpose is protecting company assets from internal and external theft and reducing shrink.

This supports a distinction between the official exit-check explanation and Costco's broader loss-prevention operation. It does **not** prove the receipt check has zero theft-deterrence value.

**Source:** S04, S05  
**Confidence:** High for job duties; Medium for the editorial inference

### C05 — Costco's retail model emphasizes high sales volume, rapid inventory turnover, and lower gross margins
Costco's FY2026 10-K says its model combines low prices, high sales volumes, rapid inventory turnover, volume purchasing, efficient distribution, reduced handling, and no-frills/self-service warehouses, allowing it to operate profitably at significantly lower gross margins than most retailers.

**Source:** S03  
**Confidence:** High

### C06 — FY2026 scale
For the 52 weeks ended August 30, 2026:
- Net sales: **$297.247 billion**
- Merchandise costs: **$264.279 billion**
- Gross margin: **$32.968 billion**
- Membership-fee revenue: **$5.907 billion**
- Net income: **$9.226 billion**

The merchandise gross margin implied by net sales and merchandise costs is about **11.1%**. Treat this as arithmetic from reported figures, not a separate company quote.

**Source:** S03, S06  
**Confidence:** High

### C07 — Membership scale and renewal
At the end of FY2026 Costco reported:
- **84.1 million paid members**
- **150.4 million total cardholders**
- U.S./Canada renewal rate: **92.3%**
- Worldwide renewal rate: **89.8%**
- Standard U.S. annual membership fee: **$65**

**Source:** S03  
**Confidence:** High

## Claims we will NOT make

- "Receipt checks are not about theft at all." Not supported.
- "The door checker catches X dollars of theft per year." No reliable Costco figure found.
- "Receipt checking causes Costco's high renewal rate." No causal evidence.
- "Membership fees are pure profit." Membership fee revenue is reported revenue, not literally costless profit.
- "Costco makes most of its profit from memberships." This common simplification is too loose without careful accounting.
- "Every receipt checker compares every SKU one-by-one." Costco's public materials do not describe the exact procedure at that level.

## Story structure

1. **The weird ritual:** you already paid, so why another check?
2. **Kill the obvious answer carefully:** Costco's official explanation is accuracy, not simply suspicion.
3. **Show the control problem:** one checkout error affects both the shopper and inventory records.
4. **Zoom out to Costco economics:** enormous sales volume + rapid turnover + low merchandise margins.
5. **Add membership economics:** paid relationship and very high renewal make member trust strategically important.
6. **Separate receipt checking from loss prevention:** theft matters, but Costco has dedicated LP functions too.
7. **Payoff:** the door check is a small second control point inside a much larger operating system.
